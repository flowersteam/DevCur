## Purpose

Metacognition — thinking about one's own thinking — is central to human curiosity and self-regulated learning. MetaCog asks whether deep RL agents can similarly represent and reason about their own epistemic states.

## Method

In collaboration with the University of Trier's Developmental Psychology lab, we ran a series of structured play studies with children aged 3–8. Key manipulations included:

- Presence vs. absence of a socially responsive robot
- Contingent vs. non-contingent peer feedback on exploration
- Scaffolded vs. open-ended task environments

We built an agent whose curiosity signal is modulated by a learned model of social attention — the agent prefers goals that it predicts will attract or maintain a partner's gaze.

## Key Findings

Social contingency significantly amplified intrinsic motivation in children and led to broader exploration. The computational model reproduced these effects and predicted individual differences in exploration breadth.
